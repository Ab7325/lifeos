# I turned my to-do list into a Pokémon game and now I actually do my to-do list

Quick confession before anything else: I'm not a proper developer. I vibecode. I tell an AI what I want, argue with it, break stuff, and at some point something works. Somewhere in the last couple of months that turned into the most fun I've had with a computer in ages, and I want to talk about why.

## Habit apps never stuck for me

I've got a lot going on that I like and a lot going on that I feel guilty about. LeetCode, gym, guitar, calisthenics, sleeping at a normal hour (that one's a running joke). Then the small stuff, like reading, protein, skincare, cutting my nails.

Every habit tracker I tried did the same thing. Here's your list, tick it, done. Day 40 looked exactly like day 1. A streak is only a number going up, and I stop caring about numbers going up pretty quickly.

Games don't have that problem. Nobody has to talk me into grinding in a game, because something small happens every few minutes. So I wondered if my actual life could pay out like that.

## Life OS, which looks boring on purpose

First thing I made was Life OS. One screen, a checklist, you tick stuff. I made it loud and ugly (thick black borders, flat colours, big chunky shadows) because every clean minimal app I'd used made me feel like I was filling in a tax form.

![Life OS daily checklist](IMAGE_LIFEOS)

Ticked rows go green and get struck through. That's it. It's a tiny bit of feedback and it's already more satisfying than a couple of apps I've actually paid for.

One thing in here I'm kind of proud of: when a month ends, it gets sealed. Written down, locked, can't be changed. Sounds minor, but it means I can delete a habit I've given up on and my history doesn't quietly rewrite itself. The past stays put.

## Then a checklist wasn't enough

"Gym" isn't one tick. Neither is guitar. So those got their own apps, and they all talk to Life OS through one shared log of events. No clever integration, they just read and write the same list.

**Strong** is my gym log. I wanted something small that did the one loop I care about: pick a routine, see what I did last time, beat it. If I hit the top of the rep range on every set, it tells me to add 2.5 kg. When I finish a workout Life OS ticks "Gym" by itself, which I never get tired of.

**Arbor** is a skill tree for calisthenics, loosely inspired by a site I liked where you pan around one huge canvas. Log a PR, a skill lights up, the next one unlocks. There are 313 skills in it. I'm never finishing it. That's sort of the appeal.

**Woodshed** is a guitar coach. It gives me the same short session every day until I genuinely own something, then it swaps in the next thing. I'm working towards Bone Bottom, Bilewater, Theme of Laura and the Mantis Lords theme from Hollow Knight, and the whole course is built backwards from those tabs, so every drill is there because some bar of a song needs it.

**Vitals** reads my band's sleep, steps and heart rate. It's the newest, and the one I've trusted with the least real data so far, so I'll say no more.

## The Pokédex is where it clicked

This bit actually changed how I behave.

Everything I do across those apps feeds one partner Pokémon. Right now it's a Charmeleon. Real work gives it XP, and the weights are deliberate: a LeetCode session or a gym day is worth around a hundred, skincare is worth one. If I tick every single habit in a day, the wild Pokémon that's been hanging around gets caught. Doing the right kind of work chips away at gym leaders. There's a weekly boss too, and it only goes down if I put in real sets and real practice.

![The Pokédex home screen](IMAGE_POKEDEX)

And I can't do anything inside the Pokédex. There's no button that earns XP. It just reads the log and works the whole game out from scratch every time you open it. So the only way forward is to actually do the day. I decided that early and everything since has had to obey it.

What I didn't see coming is how much I'd care about a pixel lizard. There've been mornings where I'm tired and halfway to skipping guitar, and then I remember an egg needs steps to hatch, or a leader is two good days from falling, and I pick the guitar up. It's silly. It works anyway.

## Stuff I got wrong

I made Mega Stones far too easy to get at first, so I had more of them than Pokémon who could use one, and had to go back and make them rarer. I built a whole Trainer tab and never opened it once, so that's gone. And Life OS started with nineteen habits where fourteen were tiny chores, so the things that mattered got buried in a wall of checkboxes. Still sorting that out.

Also, the AI doesn't magically have taste. The parts that work are the ones where I knew how I wanted the day to feel and said so clearly. The parts that flopped were the ones where I let it keep adding features because adding features is easy.

## Why I'd say go build your own

Vibecoding is best when you build something nobody else needs. Nobody else needs my exact setup, and that's the whole fun of it. I get the weird thing that's in my head, I test it on myself the next morning, and if it's annoying I change it that same night.

If there's a routine you keep dropping, make the tool for it yourself and make it a bit ridiculous. Mine has a Charmeleon in it. Yours doesn't need to.

Anyway, there's a boss waiting. Going to do some pull-ups.
